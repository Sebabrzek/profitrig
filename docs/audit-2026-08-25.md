## 0. BIGGEST GAP — leased drivers' revenue is overstated (not from the audit)

Raised by Sebastian after the audit. This outranks every bug below.

**Leased-on owner-operators are paid a percentage of the load, not the load.**
D. Lewis Transport pays 80%. ProfitRig currently treats the rate confirmation's
numbers as the driver's revenue:

```
revenue = linehaul_pay + fuel_surcharge + accessorials   // lib/loads.ts
```

So a $2,000 load shows $2,000 of revenue when the driver actually receives
$1,600. On a three-load week at $2,000 each, week profit is overstated by
**$1,200** — and overstated profit is the dangerous direction: it makes
unprofitable loads look acceptable, which is the exact failure ProfitRig exists
to prevent.

`driver_profiles.authority_type` already distinguishes leased / own_mc / both,
so the affected population is measurable today on /admin — check the "By
authority" chart before sizing this.

### Blast radius

Revenue feeds everything, so this is not a small patch:

| Touches | Why |
|---|---|
| `computeLoadEconomics` | `revenue`, `profit`, `rpm`, `profitPerMile` |
| `aggregateWeek` | week revenue / profit / RPM |
| `realCPMFromLoads` (`app/page.tsx`) | feeds the "update my estimate" insight |
| Both CSV exports | revenue and profit columns, TOTALS rows |
| `aggregateRevenue` (`lib/tax/report.ts`) | **see the 1099 problem below** |
| `LoadForm` | new input, live preview |
| `CHAT_SYSTEM_PROMPT` | bot must explain pay percentage correctly |

### Three questions to settle BEFORE building

**(a) What does the percentage apply to?** Carriers differ. Some pay a
percentage of linehaul only and pass 100% of fuel surcharge through; some take a
percentage of the all-in rate; accessorials (detention, layover, tarping) are
often passed through whole. Getting this wrong reintroduces the same error in a
smaller denomination. Ask what D. Lewis actually does, then decide whether the
model needs per-component percentages or one number is honest enough.

**(b) Where does the percentage live?** A driver is leased to one carrier at a
time, so `driver_profiles` is the natural home (`carrier_name` is already
there) — but percentages can differ by load type or change when they switch
carriers. Likely a profile default with a per-load override, mirroring how
`fuel_actual` overrides the fuel estimate.

**(c) DOUBLE-COUNT TRAP — settlement deductions vs. fixed costs.** Carriers
deduct insurance, trailer rent, ELD, escrow, and fuel advances from the
settlement. A leased driver may ALSO have entered insurance, trailer payment,
and ELD in the calculator's fixed costs. If both are counted the driver is
charged twice for the same bills. This is the same shape as the meals/per-diem
and fuel double-counts — settle it in the data model before writing code, not
after.

### The 1099 problem (two-lens rule)

For tax, a leased driver's revenue is **their share**, not the load's gross —
that is what the carrier's 1099 will show. `aggregateRevenue` currently sums the
raw pay fields, so a leased driver's Tax Pack would report more income than
their 1099. Whatever shape this takes, the tax lens must report the driver's
actual settlement revenue.

### Related

The competitive report already flagged **settlement entry** as the biggest
conversion blocker vs ProfitGauges. Percentage pay and settlements are the same
problem seen from two angles — worth designing together rather than twice.

Also relevant to invoicing: leased drivers never invoice (they get settlements),
so the same "By authority" number decides whether invoicing is worth building.

## Bugs, in the order I'd fix them

### 1. CRITICAL — Load form invites meals into the tax report
`src/app/loads/LoadForm.tsx:455`

```
label="Lumpers / parking / meals (not reimbursed)"
```

`lumpers_actual` flows into `buildScheduleCGroups` as Schedule C line 27a
(`src/lib/tax/report.ts:73,141`). A driver following this label puts food in,
and it reaches the accountant **on top of** the per-diem worksheet — the exact
double-count the road-expense Food rule was built to prevent. Two screens
currently teach opposite rules.

**Fix:** relabel to "Lumpers / detention", add a hint pointing food at the
Loads tab's Food expense. Consider a note in the tax export that
`lumpers_actual` must not contain meals. Update `CHAT_SYSTEM_PROMPT` too.

### 2. HIGH — Tolls field says $0.00 while charging the estimate
`src/app/loads/LoadForm.tsx:446-452`

```
label="Tolls"  hint="Leave blank if none."  estimate={0}
```

Regression from the tolls-parity fix (`lib/loads.ts:268-271`): a blank field now
falls back to `totalMiles × tolls_misc_per_mile`, but the form still shows
"Estimated $0.00". On a 1,000-mi load at $0.10/mi the visible line items come up
$100 short of the displayed Cost, and the hint tells drivers to do the thing
that triggers it.

**Fix:** `estimate={e.tollsIsEstimated ? e.tollsCost : 0}` (mirror the Fuel
field at line 443). Hint → "Blank = estimated from your tolls/mile rate. Enter 0
if you paid none." Consider adding tolls to the auto-allocated breakdown.
`e.tollsIsEstimated` already exists and is unused here.

### 3. HIGH — CSV formula injection (both exports)
`src/app/api/loads/export/route.ts:73` and the tax export's `csvEscape`

Guards `"` `\n` `\r` but not a leading `=` `+` `-` `@` `TAB`. A broker named
`=HYPERLINK("http://evil/","TQL")` executes when the driver's **accountant**
opens the file in Excel.

**Fix:** in both `csvEscape` functions, before the quote check —
if `/^[=+\-@\t\r]/` matches, prefix with `'`. Numbers go through `num()` and are
unaffected.

### 4. HIGH — Server-side UTC dates (three places)
Both `loads/page.tsx` and `loads/new/page.tsx` are **server components**, so
`todayIso()` runs on Vercel's UTC clock.

| Where | Symptom |
|---|---|
| `loads/page.tsx:83` week resolution | Sunday 6:30pm PT shows next week, empty — their week is behind the Prev arrow |
| `loads/page.tsx:333` road-expense default date | Denver 7pm Aug 31 → expense dated Sep 1, lands in next month's tax bucket |
| `loads/new/page.tsx:58` new-load default | Atlanta 8:30pm Sunday → load lands in the wrong week and wrong fixed-cost month |

**Fix:** one coherent timezone pass, not three patches. Either store the
driver's timezone on their profile, or resolve "today" client-side
(`RoadExpenseCard` and `LoadForm` are both already client components) with the
server value kept only as SSR fallback.

### 5. HIGH — `EMPTY_LOAD.load_date` frozen at module load
`src/lib/loads.ts:21`, used at `src/app/loads/new/page.tsx:122`

```
load_date: todayIso()          // evaluated ONCE when the module loads
...
load_date: params.date || EMPTY_LOAD.load_date
```

A warm serverless instance serves a default date from whenever it booted —
potentially days stale. Compounds #4. (The automated refuter dismissed this one;
it was wrong. Verified by hand.)

### 6. HIGH — Week CSV export disagrees with the Loads tab
`src/app/api/loads/export/route.ts:218`

The week branch fetches only that week's loads (lines 173-177, 186-193) then
builds `monthStatsByLoad` from that subset — so `buildMtdContext` gets week-only
miles and the week's earliest day as `firstLoggedDay`. `loads/page.tsx:94-116`
correctly widens to `startOfMonth(weekStart)..endOfMonth(weekEnd)`.

Same load, two different profits — roughly $840 across a week, and the TOTALS
row inherits it. In a light week the CSV can silently drop below the 1,000-mi
threshold and flip to `monthly_estimate` while the screen uses `actual_mtd`.
Month and all-time exports are unaffected.

**Fix:** in the week branch, query the full surrounding months, build
`monthStats` from that superset, emit rows only for loads inside the week.
Add a check in `tests/money.ts` asserting a week-subset context equals the
full-month context for the same load.

### 7. MEDIUM — Chat prompt teaches the old allocation rule
`src/lib/supportChat.ts:41`

Still says allocation switches to actuals at "1,000 logged MTD miles." That rule
is gone — it's now ≥7 observed days from the first logged load, with a projected
month pace ≥1,000 mi, spread over the projected month rather than miles-to-date.
The bot will confidently misinform drivers about their own numbers.

## Suggestions worth acting on

### Cost-per-mile comprehension (the stated #1 problem)

- **Explain CPM as a load, not an abstraction.** One line under the big number,
  from data already computed: *"A 600-mile load has to pay you at least $1,122
  or you're hauling it for free. To make your $0.50/mi profit, don't book it
  under $1,422."* — copy only.
- **Warn when driver pay is $0.** `DEFAULTS` sets it to 0 (`page.tsx:35`). A
  driver who leaves it there gets a CPM that looks beatable at cheap rates — the
  classic trap of buying yourself a job. *"This assumes you drive for free."*
- **Show the fixed/variable split.** `Calculator.tsx` computes `fixedPerMile`
  and `variablePerMile` then discards the split. That split IS the lesson:
  "bills that come whether you drive or not" vs "what each mile burns."
- **Miles what-if strip** under Monthly Miles: `8,000 → $2.31 | 10,000 → $2.10 |
  12,000 → $1.96`, headed *"sit a week and your truck payment doesn't shrink —
  it piles onto fewer miles."*
- **Fresh visitors see "$0.95 — your true cost per mile" with every fixed cost
  at zero.** That number is a lie until they enter their bills. Flag it.

### Data entry

- **Duplicate a load + broker autocomplete** (small, do first). `LoadForm`
  starts from `EMPTY_LOAD` every time; owner-ops run the same lanes weekly.
- **"What did it pay?" as one total field**, with linehaul/FSC/accessorials
  behind a "split it out" link. Leased drivers often only know the settlement
  total and have to invent a split.
- **Diesel price goes stale silently** — set once, drives every load's fuel
  estimate forever. Show its age, make it editable from the load form.

### Rate confirmations — sequencing (this was thought through, not guessed)

1. **In-app upload first.** Button on the load form → PDF/photo → Claude
   extracts → prefilled `LoadForm` for review. API key already exists, no new
   infrastructure, no new trust ask. Proves extraction accuracy before you
   invest in plumbing.
2. **Dedicated forward-to address second** (`loads-a1b2c3@in.profitrig.com`).
   Inbound-parse webhook via Resend. Results MUST land in a review queue, never
   auto-create.
3. **Inbox access: don't.** Broad OAuth scopes on an app already holding their
   financial data — big trust ask and breach liability to save a forward tap.

Note: rate cons carry broker, origin, destination, date and pay — but rarely
**miles**, the most load-bearing field. Worth knowing before building.

**Flagged as traps:** in-cab voice (engine noise; a misheard $2,150 → $21.50
silently corrupts profit) and ELD integration (per-vendor partner programs,
months of approval). The monthly odometer checkpoint captures most of the
mileage value for a fraction of the effort.
